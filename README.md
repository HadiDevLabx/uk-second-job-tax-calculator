# Second Job Tax Calculator UK — How Is a Second Job Taxed?

> A second job has no special tax rate. It is usually taxed on a BR code, meaning 20% from the first pound, because your personal allowance is already used by the first job.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

Your £12,570 personal allowance is normally allocated to one employment. The second gets a **BR** code and every pound is taxed at basic rate, or **D0** at higher rate if your main job already fills the basic band.

National Insurance is worked out **separately by each employer**, on their own payments. Neither knows about the other, so you can pay the 8% main rate twice on earnings that would have been 2% in one job.

If both jobs are small, you can ask HMRC to split the allowance between them.

## What this does not cover

A BR code is not necessarily wrong. It only over-deducts if your combined income is below the personal allowance, which HMRC will correct once it sees both employments.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
